Why Soil Health Matters

The Ground Beneath Your Lawn Determines Everything

Most lawn problems — brown patches, thinning grass, persistent weeds, poor plant growth — trace back to the soil. If the soil is compacted, poorly drained, nutrient-depleted, or biologically dead, nothing you put on top will fix it for long.

Bluffton and the Lowcountry present a particularly varied soil environment. Some properties have sandy, fast-draining soil with low nutrient holding capacity. Others have dense clay layers that trap water and cut off oxygen to roots. Many newer developments were built on compacted builder fill with little to no organic matter.

InfiniteAg's soil health assessment evaluates all of this before recommending any treatment or amendment. You'll come away with a clear picture of what your soil actually needs — not a generic fertilizer program designed for the average lawn.

  • Soil compaction assessment and aeration recommendations
  • Drainage evaluation and recommendations
  • Nutrient availability and pH analysis
  • Organic matter and soil biology evaluation
  • Yard-specific improvement recommendations

Soil Compaction

Compacted soil prevents water, air, and nutrients from reaching roots. Common in Bluffton properties with high foot traffic or heavy construction equipment.

Drainage Problems

Poor drainage leads to root suffocation, fungal disease, and standing water. Knowing why drainage fails is the first step to fixing it.

Nutrient Imbalance

Sandy Lowcountry soils often lack nitrogen, phosphorus, and micronutrients. Over-fertilizing the wrong elements compounds the problem.

Soil Biology

Beneficial microbes, earthworms, and fungi are essential for healthy soil. Without them, organic matter doesn't break down and nutrients stay unavailable.

What We Look At

What InfiniteAg Evaluates During a Soil Assessment

A complete soil health picture requires looking at physical structure, drainage behavior, chemical balance, and biological activity.

Physical Soil Structure

Texture (sandy, loamy, clay), compaction levels, and whether roots can penetrate and expand. Bluffton's varied soil types require different approaches depending on your property's specific conditions.

Water Movement & Drainage

How quickly water absorbs, where it pools, and whether layers of impermeable soil or clay are blocking drainage below the surface. This affects everything from irrigation scheduling to disease pressure.

pH & Nutrient Availability

Soil pH determines which nutrients are available to plants. Even if nutrients are present, the wrong pH locks them out. The Lowcountry's sandy soils often trend toward lower pH, which affects nutrient uptake in warm-season grasses.

Organic Matter Content

Organic matter feeds soil microbes, improves water retention, and makes nutrients available to plant roots. Many Bluffton properties — especially newer construction — have very little.

Root Zone Depth

How deep roots can actually grow matters as much as what's at the surface. Hardpan layers and compaction can limit root depth, making grass far more vulnerable to drought and disease.

Specific Amendment Plan

Based on what we find, we recommend targeted amendments — compost, specific fertilizers, lime, aeration, or drainage modifications — rather than a generic program that may not address your actual issues.

Questions

Soil Health Assessment — FAQ

Do I need a soil test before getting a soil health assessment?

A lab soil test can provide useful data on pH and nutrient levels, and we can help interpret those results. However, a soil health assessment from InfiniteAg goes beyond lab numbers — we also evaluate physical compaction, drainage behavior, organic matter, and overall growing conditions on-site. We can recommend a soil test as part of the assessment process if needed.

My lawn looks fine — do I still need a soil assessment?

A soil assessment is valuable even for lawns that appear healthy, especially if you're applying fertilizer regularly or planning to add edible gardens or new plantings. It confirms that what you're doing is working — or reveals that you're applying products the soil doesn't need. Many soil problems show up gradually before becoming obvious in the lawn.
